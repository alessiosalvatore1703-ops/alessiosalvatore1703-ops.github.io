# Writing rules for tailored CV bullets

Read this before rewording any entry. The goal: a recruiter or PI reading the CV next
to their own posting should feel the fit without spotting a single echoed phrase.

## Re-frame, don't parrot

- Never lift more than ~3 consecutive words from the job description. If the posting
  says "multi-sensor fusion for legged robots", the bullet does not say "multi-sensor
  fusion" — it shows it: "fused LiDAR-inertial odometry with YOLO detections…".
- Do not rename projects or roles into the posting's jargon. Titles stay as the
  master CV has them.
- Skills emerge from bullets, not from a keyword list. No "Skills: Python, ROS, …"
  section stuffed to match the posting.

## Lead with the facet the role needs

Each kept entry has several true facets; put the relevant one first. The same OMAV
project leads with "deployed zero-shot on the real OMAV" for a hardware/controls role,
and with "distilled … through differentiable dynamics with DAgger" for an ML role.
Reordering and re-emphasizing true facts is the whole craft — changing the facts is
forbidden.

## Keep the master CV's voice

- Past-tense action verbs, dense noun phrases, metric + qualifier
  ("79% of point-goal success … (20 unseen episodes)").
- One idea per bullet. If a merged bullet needs two "and"s and a semicolon to hold
  together, it is two bullets or one dropped clause.
- Drop before you compress into mush: a cut entry is invisible; a mangled bullet is not.

## Honesty checklist (apply to every reworded bullet)

- Is every number in a source file (`content/projects/*.md` or `main.tex`)?
- Did the qualifier travel with the claim ("in simulation", "validation",
  "qualitative only")?
- Is team work still recognizably team work?
- Would the flags section of the project's content file permit this sentence?
